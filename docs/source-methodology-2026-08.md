# Source methodology research: what each upstream documents about itself

Research session date: **2026-08-18 (UTC)**. Every value below comes from a page fetched during this session. Where a page could not be reached, or is silent on the question, the cell reads `n.a.` **Nothing here is filled in from general knowledge**, and nothing is inferred from a source's behaviour — only from what its publisher writes down.

## Why this document exists

`sources.yaml` assigns every source a weight, an independence class, and — for two of them — the right to promote an indicator to high confidence on its own. Those are consequential choices, and until now the reasoning behind them lived partly in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) and partly in the heads of whoever made them. This document records **what each publisher actually documents about where its data comes from and how any score of its own is computed**, so that our weighting can be traced to a documented upstream methodology rather than to an assumption about one.

Two framing notes, because this table is easy to misread.

**A missing methodology page is not a defect.** Most of these feeds are run by volunteers or by small teams giving away work for free. Writing and maintaining documentation is unpaid labour on top of unpaid labour, and a publisher who ships good data and no prose has still done the community a service. `n.a.` in this table means *we could not find it in this session* — nothing more.

**A source with no published score is not a worse source.** A binary "this netblock is criminal-controlled" list produced by human investigation can be far more precise than a numeric score derived from volume. Feodo Tracker and Spamhaus DROP are the two sources permitted to promote alone in this project, and neither publishes a numeric score at all. Presence in a well-defined set is itself a strong statement.

**Corrections are welcome.** If you publish one of these sources and this page misstates your method, or misses documentation that exists, open an issue or a pull request and it will be fixed. Correcting a cell here is a documentation change, not an argument.

## What this changes about how we read our own inputs

Four findings from this session bear directly on configuration.

**1. Only four upstreams publish a per-indicator score, and only three describe how it is derived.** AbuseIPDB (log of distinct reports, time-decayed, recalculated daily), StopForumSpam (last-seen date plus sighting count, as a percentage), and IPsum (count of blacklist occurrences) each state a basis. IPThreat states a direction — recent submissions raise the level, the level decays — without a scale. ThreatFox returns a `confidence_level` whose derivation is not published. Everything else is a membership list.

This is the documented justification for a design choice that was previously just an instinct: **we do not compose upstream scores, because most upstreams do not have one, and the ones that do are not measuring the same quantity.** A percentage derived from forum-spam sightings and a logarithm of distinct abuse reports cannot be averaged into anything meaningful. Our score is therefore derived from *independent presence*, which is the one signal every source in the set actually provides.

**2. Two sources document a verification step, and those are the two that may promote alone.** Feodo Tracker adds an address "only… if it responds with a valid botnet C2 response" — an active check, not a report count. Spamhaus DROP lists only after "investigators and forensics specialists have gathered evidence", re-evaluated daily. Both promotion exceptions in `sources.yaml` now trace to a documented upstream method rather than to reputation. Nothing else in the set documents a comparable verification step, and nothing else should acquire that privilege without one.

**3. Retention behaviour varies by more than an order of magnitude, and it is mostly documented.** Blocklist.de publishes rolling 48-hour windows. Feodo Tracker's blocklist covers active C2s or those seen within 30 days. AbuseIPDB decays report weight continuously. IPThreat's level decays over time. Artillery, which produces the Binary Defense banlist, "blacklists them forever" for a honeypot connection. That last one is worth carrying forward: a source whose upstream tool never expires an entry will accumulate stale addresses over time regardless of how sound its detection is, which is the same property that keeps `sefinek` disabled (ADR-048). It is not a reason to drop Binary Defense — it *is* a reason its records should never outlive our own TTL, which they do not.

**4. bruteforceblocker's tooling is unmaintained while its data is current.** The FreeBSD port is marked "DEPRECATED: Abandonware, please consider using security/sshguard" and expired 2022-03-31. The published list, however, was still updating daily as of the licence research session. These are two different facts and should not be conflated: **the packaging is abandoned; the feed is alive.** The practical consequence is that the reporting population may shrink over time as operators migrate to sshguard, so its class weight should be revisited if its volume declines rather than assumed stable.

## Corrections applied during this session

Recorded so the same errors are not reintroduced.

- **Binary Defense was previously treated as having no documented origin.** It does — Artillery is open source and describes its honeypot and log-monitoring behaviour in detail. The gap was that the documentation lives in the tool's repository rather than beside the feed.
- **Feodo Tracker was previously treated as having no documented methodology.** Its blocklist page documents an active-verification inclusion rule and its retention window, which is exactly the evidence that supports its promotion privilege.
- **IPThreat was previously treated as publishing no score.** Its FAQ documents threat-level behaviour, which also confirms our reading of the `threat-N.txt` filenames as minimum-score slices rather than time windows (ADR-050).
- **bruteforceblocker's deprecation was previously stated too broadly.** The port is abandonware; the data is not.

## Open questions for publishers

Asked here in the same spirit as the licence questions in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) — offered as questions, not complaints.

- **ThreatFox**: is the derivation of `confidence_level` documented anywhere public? We currently ignore the value and treat presence as the signal.
- **CINS**: is a scale or range published for the CINS Score? We consume the Army list as a membership set and do not attempt to interpret a score.
- **IPThreat**: is there a published definition of the threat-level values, so a minimum-score choice can be reasoned about rather than measured empirically?
- **DataPlane.org**: is there a methodology page describing how a signal is qualified, beyond the node-count summary on the front page?
- **Turris Sentinel**: is the greylist inclusion or aging rule documented publicly?

Any of these being answered would improve this project's weighting. None of them being answered is a perfectly reasonable state of affairs for a free public feed.
